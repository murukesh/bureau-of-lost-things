## Chapter 35 — The Marble That Knew Which Way Was Down

Inside the house, the pull was a thing you could not think in, and the not-thinking was the problem, because the plan needed thinking, and the pull was a thing that ate thinking, and the pull was a thing that made you want to go uphill, toward the Keeper, toward the keeping, toward the cold, and the wanting was not your wanting, and the wanting was the Keeper's wanting, and the Keeper had learned to want, and the Keeper wanted you to want to be kept.

"Think," said Pip, and he was leaning into the pull, and his feet were sliding, and his coat was pulling, and his buttons were pulling, and he was thinking, he was trying to think, but the pull was a thing that ate thinking. "Think. The plan. The marble. The marble finds the off. The marble finds the heart. The marble… the marble…" He looked at the rooms, at the keeping room, at the rooms that the outside of the house did not have room for, and he could not see the heart. He could not see the way. The pull was a thing that made everything lean uphill, and the leaning was a thing that made everything look the same, and the same was a thing that made you lost, and the lost was a thing that the Keeper wanted, because the Keeper wanted everything to be lost, because the Keeper wanted everything to be kept.

"Goldie," said Pip. "The marble. The marble. Roll the marble."

And Goldie, who was leaning into the pull, who was holding the pocket shut with both hands, who was fighting the wanting, the wanting that was not her wanting, the wanting that was the Keeper's wanting, took out the marble. The cat's-eye. The swirl of green and gold. The chip from the church steps. The six years of useless. The six years of ready.

"The marble," said Goldie. "The marble's glass. The marble's not brass. The marble doesn't answer to the brass. The marble doesn't get pulled." She set the marble on the floor. And the marble did not lean uphill. The marble did not want to be kept. The marble did not answer to the brass. The marble was glass. And the glass was free.

And the marble rolled.

It rolled the way a marble rolls, which is to say it rolled true, which is to say it rolled the way the floor went, and the floor went toward the heart, because the floor of the house leaned toward the heart, because the heart was the thing that was pulling, and the floor was the thing that was being pulled, and the floor leaned toward the heart. And the marble rolled toward the heart. And the marble rolled true. And the marble did not lean the way the pull wanted it to lean. The marble rolled the way the floor actually went. And the floor actually went toward the heart. And the marble showed them the way.

"Follow the marble," said Pip. "Follow the marble. The marble knows which way is down. The marble knows which way is the heart. The marble… the marble's not pulled. The marble's free. And the free thing shows the way. Follow the marble."

And they followed the marble. They followed the marble through the keeping room, through the rooms that the outside of the house did not have room for, through the pull, through the wanting, through the not-thinking, and the marble rolled true, and the marble rolled toward the heart, and the marble showed them the way. And the pull was a thing that ate thinking, but the marble was a thing that did not think, and the not-thinking was the thing that saved them, because the marble did not think, and the marble did not want, and the marble did not answer to the brass, and the marble rolled true, and the true was the way.

And the marble rolled to the heart.

The heart of the house was a room, and the room was the Keeper, and the Keeper was brass and gears and springs and little copper wires, and it was the size of a room, and it was growing, and it was pulling, and it was keeping everything, and it did not know when to stop. And in the middle of the Keeper, in the very heart of it, there was a jar. A small glass jar, set into the brass, held there by the gears, the way a heart is held by the ribs. And in the jar there was something small. Something metal. Something that caught the lamplight. A collar tag. A dog's collar tag. Biscuit's collar tag. The first thing. The thing that started it. The thing the Keeper was built around.

And the marble rolled to the jar. The marble rolled to the heart. And the marble stopped, because the marble had found the thing it was looking for, because the marble had found the off, because the off was the heart, because the off was the jar, because the off was the love, because the off was the thing the Keeper was built around, and the thing the Keeper was built around was the thing that could make it let go.

"There," said Goldie. "There. The marble found it. The marble found the heart. The marble found the off." She looked at the jar, at the small metal thing, at the collar tag, and she said, "The off is the love. The off is the thing the Keeper is built around. The off is… the off is the dog. The off is Biscuit. The off is the love stays." She looked at Snell, at the man shaped like an umbrella, and she said, "Mr. Snell. The off is the love. The off is the thing you built it around. The off is… the off is letting go. The off is letting the love stay, even when the thing goes. The off is you. The off is you letting go."

And Snell, who was a man shaped like an umbrella, who had been alone eight years, who had built a machine so nothing he loves is ever lost again, looked at the jar, at the collar tag, at the first thing, at the thing that started it, and the crack opened, wider than it had ever been, and for a long moment he did not speak. And then he said, "Biscuit. Biscuit. I… I built it around Biscuit. I built it around… around the love. And the love… the love is still there. The love is still there. Even after eight years. Even with the curtains closed. The love is still there."

"The love stays," said Pip. "The love stays. Even when the thing is gone. The love stays. And the Keeper is built around the love. And the Keeper does not know that the love stays without the keeping. And we're going to show it. We're going to show it the love stays. And we're going to make it let go."

And the marble had saved the day. The marble had found the heart. The marble had found the off. The marble had shown them the way, because the marble was glass, and the glass was free, and the free thing showed the way. And the marble was useless. And the marble was ready. And the marble had been useless for six years so it could be ready for this job. And the marble had done the job. And the marble had saved the day.

And Goldie picked up the marble, and she held it, and she said, "Good marble. Good marble. You did the job. You did the job." And she put the marble back in the pocket, and the pocket jingled the small, satisfied jingle, and the marble was spent, and the marble was ready for the next thing, and the next thing was the paperclip, and the next thing was the button, and the next thing was the rubber band, and the next thing was the socks.

But the marble had done the job. The marble had found the heart. The marble had found the off. And the off was the love. And the off was the letting go. And the off was Snell.

And the Keeper hummed, and the Keeper pulled, and the Keeper was keeping everything, and it did not know when to stop.

But the marble had found the heart. And the heart was the thing they needed. And the heart was the thing they were going to answer.

And the next thing was the paperclip. And the next thing was the button. And the next thing was the rubber band. And the next thing was the socks.

But that was later. That was the next chapter. That was the answer.

For now, there was a marble that had found the heart, and a jar with a collar tag, and a man who was ready to try to let go.

For now, that was enough.
