## Chapter 37 — The Sock Trail Home

The Keeper was jammed, but the Keeper was still holding, and the holding was a thing that needed a letting go, and the letting go was a thing that needed a lead, and the lead was the socks, and the socks were the lonely socks, and the lonely socks were the homing things, and the homing things were the thing that led the way home.

"Thimble," said Pip. "The socks. The lonely socks. Release the socks. Let them go home. Let them lead the way."

And Thimble, who sorted the socks, who gave spare buttons to lost coats, who was prepared for loss, went to the bin, the special bin, the one he had labeled READY / GOING HOME / SMELLS LIKE SOMEONE, and he opened it, and the lonely socks were there, the single socks, the ones that smelled like toast and rain and grass and someone else's house, and they were leaning, all of them, leaning toward the green door, leaning toward the way home, leaning toward the pairs, leaning toward the people, leaning toward the home.

"They want home," said Thimble. "They want home more than anything. More than a glove wants its glove. More than a key wants its lock. A lonely sock is a homing thing. And a homing thing does not stay kept. A homing thing finds its way." He picked up a sock, a small gray one, and he held it to his nose, and he closed his eyes, and he said, "Toast. This one smells like toast. This one… this one is the loneliest. This one wants home the most." And he held up the sock, and it was the duck sock. The duck sock. Ollie's duck sock. The one from the dryer. The one that smelled like toast. The one that had gone missing from the Bureau. The one that had been kept.

"The duck sock," said Ollie. "The duck sock. My duck sock. My duck sock. It's… it's here. It's here. It's here." And he reached for it, and Thimble held it up, and he said, "The duck sock goes first. The duck sock is the loneliest. The duck sock wants home the most. And the duck sock leads the way. The duck sock leads the way home."

"But the Keeper," said Goldie. "The Keeper is still holding. The Keeper is jammed, but the Keeper is still holding. The socks… the socks can't get out. The Keeper is holding the way. The Keeper is holding the… the grip. The socks can't get past the grip."

And she was right. The Keeper was jammed, but the Keeper was still holding, and the holding was a grip, a brass grip, a thing that held the way, and the socks could not get past the grip, and the socks could not get out, and the socks could not lead the way home, because the grip was holding the way.

"The rubber band," said Goldie. "The rubber band. The rubber band launches. The rubber band launches the first sock. The rubber band launches the duck sock. The rubber band launches the duck sock past the grip. The rubber band launches the duck sock out the door. And the duck sock leads the way. And the other socks follow. And the trail starts. And the trail leads home." She took out the rubber band. The red one. The cracked one. The stretched one. The one that had held her hair the day she learned to ride a bike. The one that had been cracked for years so it could be ready for this job. "The rubber band's a snap. The rubber band's a launch. The rubber band's the thing that starts the trail."

And she made a slingshot. She made a slingshot the way you make a slingshot when you are twelve and you know every shortcut and your pocket has been knowing things for years, which is to say she made it out of the rubber band and her two fingers and the duck sock, and she pulled it back, and she pulled it back, and she pulled it back, and the rubber band stretched, and the rubber band cracked, and the rubber band was about to snap, and the rubber band was ready, and the rubber band was the thing that started the trail.

"Let go," said Goldie. And she let go.

And the rubber band snapped. And the duck sock flew. The duck sock flew past the grip, past the brass, past the holding, past the keeping, and the duck sock flew out the green door, out the way home, out into the town, out into the first day of fall, and the duck sock flew home. And the duck sock flew toward Ollie. And the duck sock flew toward the pair. And the duck sock flew toward the home. And the duck sock led the way.

And the other socks followed.

The lonely socks followed the duck sock. The single socks followed the duck sock. The ones that smelled like toast and rain and grass and someone else's house followed the duck sock, and they flew past the grip, past the brass, past the holding, past the keeping, and they flew out the green door, out the way home, out into the town, and they flew home. And they flew toward their pairs. And they flew toward their people. And they flew toward the home. And they formed a trail. A trail of single socks. A trail of lonely socks. A trail of homing things. And the trail led from the house, through the green door, down the hill, into the town, and the trail led home.

And the kept things followed.

The kept things followed the socks. The music box followed the socks. The telescope followed the socks. The harmonica followed the socks. The red mitten followed the socks. The eighteen followed the socks. The nineteen followed the socks. The twenty followed the socks. The everything followed the socks. And the kept things flew out the green door, out the way home, out into the town, and they flew home. And they flew toward their people. And they flew toward the ones who loved them. And they flew toward the home. And the Keeper let go. The Keeper let go the things. The Keeper let go the grip. The Keeper let go the keeping. And the things went home. And the things went home along the sock trail. And the sock trail led the way. And the sock trail led home.

And the trail led everyone home.

Pip followed the trail. Goldie followed the trail. Ollie followed the trail. Barnaby followed the trail. Miss Whisk followed the trail. Thimble followed the trail. And Snell followed the trail. Snell, who was a man shaped like an umbrella, who had been alone eight years, who had built a machine so nothing he loves is ever lost again, followed the sock trail, and the sock trail led him out the green door, out the way home, down the hill, into the town, and the sock trail led him home. And the sock trail led him toward the bakery. And the sock trail led him toward The Warm Loaf. And the sock trail led him toward the bowl. And the sock trail led him toward the dog.

But that was later. That was the reunion. That was the next chapter.

For now, there was a sock trail, and a rubber band that had snapped, and a duck sock that had led the way, and a Keeper that had let go.

And the rubber band had saved the day. The rubber band had launched the first sock. The rubber band had started the trail. The rubber band had been cracked for years so it could be ready for this job. And the rubber band had done the job. And the rubber band had saved the day.

And the lonely socks had saved the day. The lonely socks had led the way home. The lonely socks had been homing things. And the homing things had led the way. And the trail of single socks had led everyone home. And the trail had led home.

And Goldie's pocket was empty. The marble was spent. The paperclip was spent. The rubber band was spent. The three useless things had done the job. The three useless things had saved the day. And the pocket was empty. And the pocket jingled, but the jingle was a different jingle, the jingle of a pocket that has done the job, the jingle of a pocket that is empty and is full, the jingle of a pocket that has loved and has let go.

And Goldie patted the empty pocket, and she said, "Good pocket. Good pocket. You did the job. You did the job." And she smiled, the shortcut smile, the *this is a terrible idea and I'm in* smile, and she said, "The sad's the good kind. The sad's the good kind. The kind that means it was real."

And the sock trail led home. And the sock trail led everyone home. And the sock trail led toward the bakery. And the sock trail led toward the bowl. And the sock trail led toward the dog.

But that was later. That was the reunion. That was the answer.

For now, there was a sock trail, and an empty pocket, and a Keeper that had let go, and a man following the trail home.

For now, that was enough.
