## Chapter 32 - Everything Is Missing (Including the Missing Things)

The Bureau was emptying, and the Bureau had never emptied, and the emptying was a thing that had a sound, and the sound was the sound of a waiting room becoming a room that was not waiting anymore.

Miss Whisk stamped the floor. She stamped it DO NOT, the way she had stamped it when the Keeper reached for Goldie's pocket, the way she had stamped it when the Keeper reached for the captain, and the stamp was the truth, and the truth was no. And the Keeper slowed. The Keeper slowed the way a thing slows when it is told no by a cat with a stamp. But the Keeper did not stop. The Keeper was bigger now. The Keeper was much bigger now. And the Keeper slowed, and the Keeper kept pulling, and the shelves kept emptying, and Miss Whisk stamped, and Miss Whisk stamped, and Miss Whisk stamped, and her paw was shaking, and the almost-smile was gone, and the truth was no, and the no was not enough.

"It's not enough," she said, and her voice was flat and precise and dangerous, but it was also, for the first time, a little afraid. "The stamp. The no. It's not enough. The Keeper is bigger. The Keeper is much bigger. And the no... the no is not enough." She looked at her stamp, and her paw was shaking, and she put it behind her back so nobody would see. "I have been the truth's secretary for a long time. And the truth has always been enough. And now... now the truth is not enough. And I do not know what to do when the truth is not enough."

And Barnaby, who was the Chief, who had been the Chief for a very long time, who had spent his whole life keeping things safe and had just learned that safe was not a thing you could promise, sat down on a lost step, heavily, and the step said STEP. STILL LOOKING., and Barnaby put his face in his flippers, and he made a sound that was not quite a sob and not quite a hum, a sound that was a walrus who has been the Chief for a very long time and has just learned that the no is not enough.

"I don't know," he said. "I don't know what to do. I have been the Chief for a very long time. And I have always known what to do. And now... now I don't know. The Keeper is bigger. The Keeper is much bigger. And the no is not enough. And the stamp is not enough. And the... and the waiting is not enough. And I don't know what to do." He looked up, and his small eyes were wet, and his whiskers were down, and he was a very old, very kind, very tired walrus who had just learned that the thing he had been the Chief of was not enough. "I don't know what to do."

And the Bureau was quiet. The Bureau was quiet the way a room is quiet when the person who is supposed to know what to do has just said *I don't know what to do.* And the shelves kept emptying. And the Keeper kept pulling. And the hum was loud. And the hum was proud. And the hum was the hum of a machine that had learned to want.

And then Ollie, who was six, who was the Official Counter, who was brave in small, loud ways, said, "You wait."

Barnaby looked up. "What?"

"You wait," said Ollie. "That's what you do. You wait. You said. You said a thing that is lost is not gone. It's waiting. And a thing that is waiting should not wait alone. That's what you said. That's the whole Bureau. The rest is paperwork." He looked at Barnaby with his whole face. "So you wait. You wait with the things. You wait with the things that are being taken. You wait with them. And you love them back. And you remember them. And you count them. And you... you don't let them be forgotten. That's what you do. That's the whole thing. You wait."

And Barnaby, who was the Chief, who had been the Chief for a very long time, who had just said *I don't know what to do*, looked at the six-year-old who had just said *you wait*, and the thing that had gone out of him came back, a little, just a little, just a spark, and he said, "I wait. I wait with the things. I wait with them. And I love them back. And I remember them. And I count them. And I don't let them be forgotten." He stood up, heavily, and his whiskers moved, and he said, "That's what I do. That's the whole thing. I wait. And the waiting is not nothing. The waiting is the thing. The waiting is the love. And the love stays. Even when the thing is taken. Even when the thing is kept. Even when the thing is gone. The love stays."

And Miss Whisk, who was the Head Cataloguer, who kept the record of everyone's loving, took her paw from behind her back, and she looked at her stamp, and she said, "The no is not enough. But the record is enough. The record is the truth. And the truth is that the things are loved. And the truth is that the love stays. And I am going to write it down. I am going to write down every thing that is taken, and every person who loves it, and I am going to write down that the love stays. And the record is going to be the thing that is enough. Because the record is the love. And the love stays." And she stamped the ledger, and the stamp said LOVED, and it was a new stamp, a stamp she had just made up, and it was true.

And Thimble, who sorted the socks, who gave spare buttons to lost coats, who was prepared for loss, said, "The socks. The lonely socks. They want home. They want home more than anything. And the Keeper is taking them. But the socks... the socks are homing things. And a homing thing does not stay kept. A homing thing finds its way. And the socks are going to find their way. And they're going to lead the way. And I'm going to help them." And his quills clicked, and the buttons were there, the spare buttons, the promises, and he was going to use one, and he was going to have a word.

And Goldie, who knew every shortcut, who had a pocket full of useless junk, patted the pocket, and the marble and the paperclip and the rubber band clicked together, the small, satisfied jingle, and she said, "The pocket's ready. The pocket's been ready. The pocket's been knowing things for years. And the pocket knows the how. And the how is the marble, and the paperclip, and the rubber band. And the how is the three useless things. And the how is the thing we're going to show the machine." She looked at Pip with her shortcut eyes, her kind, calm, shortcut eyes. "The pocket knows, Pip. The pocket knows the how. And we're going to listen."

And Pip, who was eleven, who had a label maker, who had a pocket full of ready things and a cat with a stamp and a walrus who waits and a hedgehog who sorts the socks and a six-year-old who counts to zero, felt the thing settle into him, hard and clear, like a label pressed onto a thing that was not going anywhere. The how was coming. The how was the pocket. The how was the socks. The how was the button. The how was the label. And the how was the love stays. And the love stays was the answer. And the answer was the thing they were going to show the machine.

"Then we go," said Pip. "We go up the hill. We go to the house. We go to the Keeper. And we answer the heartbreak. And we show it the love stays. And we make it stop." He looked at the Bureau, at the walrus and the cat and the hedgehog and the girl and the six-year-old, and he said, "Are you ready?"

"I'm ready," said Barnaby.

"I'm ready," said Miss Whisk.

"I'm ready," said Thimble.

"I'm ready," said Goldie.

"I'm ready," said Ollie, at full volume, which was his way of signing a contract. "I'm the Official Counter. I count to zero. And I'm going to count to zero. I promise."

And they went to the big door, the one at the back, the one that was the only way in or out, and Barnaby opened it, and the door opened the way a door opens when a walrus has decided to be brave, and they went out, into the town, into the first day of fall, into the bunting and the tables and the unbuttoned coats and the one-sock-on feet and the sky that was full of umbrellas.

And the town watched them go. And the town said, "Who are they?" And the town did not know. And the town was about to find out.

And under the hill, the Keeper hummed, patient and keeping, bigger now, much bigger now, and it had learned to want, and it wanted everything, and it did not know when to stop.

But the Bureau was going up the hill. The Bureau was going to answer the heartbreak. The Bureau was going to show the machine the love stays.

And the machine was going to find out that the love stays, even when the thing is kept, even when the thing is gone.

But that was the climax. That was the answer.

For now, there was a Bureau going up the hill, and a pocket full of ready things, and a six-year-old who was going to count to zero.

For now, that was enough.
