## Chapter 34 - The Hill Grew Teeth (Metaphorically)

The hill had grown teeth, metaphorically, which was the only way Pip could think of it, because the hill was pulling, and the pulling was a thing you felt in your feet, and the pulling was a thing that made the grass lean uphill, and the leaning was a thing that made the hill look like it was... like it was grabbing, like it was holding on, like it had grown teeth and the teeth were the pull and the pull was the Keeper.

They went up the hill, and the hill pulled. It pulled their coats. It pulled their buttons. It pulled the loose change in Goldie's pocket, the pennies and the paperclip and the marble, and Goldie held the pocket shut with both hands, and she said, "The hill's got teeth. The hill's got teeth. I don't like the hill's teeth." And the hill pulled, and the hill pulled, and the hill pulled, and they leaned into it, the way you lean into a wind, and they went up, and they went up, and they went up.

And the house was huge.

The house with too many curtains had grown. It had grown rooms, and rooms, and rooms, and the rooms were stacked and leaned and curved, and the house was the size of a hill now, and the house was the size of a Bureau, and the house was the size of a thing that had been keeping for eight years and had just decided to keep everything. And every window was curtained, and every curtain was drawn, and the house was holding its breath, and the breath was the hum, and the hum was loud, and the hum was proud, and the hum was the hum of a machine that had learned to want.

And Snell was at the gate.

He was shaped like an umbrella, and he was waving his long arms, and he was shouting at the house, at the Keeper, at the machine, and the machine did not listen. And when he saw them, when he saw Pip and Goldie and Ollie and Barnaby and Miss Whisk and Thimble, his face did the thing, the soft-sad thing, the thing that was a dog-shaped hole, and he said, "I can't stop it. I can't stop it. I built it, and I can't stop it. It... it learned to want. It learned to want everything. And I can't stop it. I've been shouting. I've been shouting at it. And it doesn't listen. It doesn't listen anymore. It... it used to listen. It used to hum happy when I was close. And now... now it just pulls. And I can't stop it."

"Mr. Snell," said Pip. "We're going to stop it. We're going to answer the heartbreak. We're going to show it the love stays. We're going to make it stop."

"You can't," said Snell. "You can't. It's too big. It's much too big. It's... it's keeping everything. And it's going to keep everything. And you can't... you can't stop a thing that keeps. You can't. I built it to keep. And it keeps. And it keeps. And it keeps." He looked at the house, at the rooms, at the curtained windows, and his face was the face of a man looking at a thing he has built and cannot control. "I built it to keep. And it keeps. And I can't... I can't make it let go. I can't make it let go. I've been trying. I've been trying since... since I let the dinosaur go. Since I let the captain go. And the Keeper... the Keeper kept harder. The Keeper grew. And now... now it's keeping everything. And I can't make it let go."

"Then we make it," said Pip. "We make it let go. We show it the love stays. We show it that... that keeping is not loving. That keeping is being afraid, with a machine. And we show it that the love stays, even when the thing goes. And we make it let go." He looked at Snell with his eleven-year-old eyes, his label-maker eyes, his *I will find this and I will write it down and I will make it right* eyes. "Mr. Snell. We need you. We need you to help us. We need you to... to let go. To let go with us. To let go together. Because the Keeper is built around the love. And the love is yours. And the letting go is yours. And we can't... we can't make it let go without you. We need you. We need you to let go."

And Snell, who was a man shaped like an umbrella, who had been alone eight years, who had built a machine so nothing he loves is ever lost again, looked at the boy with the label-maker eyes, and the crack opened, wider than it had ever been, and for a long moment he did not speak. And then he said, "I... I don't know how. I don't know how to let go. I've been keeping for eight years. I've been keeping. And I don't... I don't know how to let go. I don't know how."

"We'll show you," said Goldie, and she patted the pocket, and the marble and the paperclip and the rubber band clicked together, the small, satisfied jingle. "We'll show you. The pocket knows. The pocket's been knowing things for years. And the pocket knows how to let go. And we'll show you." She looked at Snell with her shortcut eyes, her kind, calm, shortcut eyes. "I love my stuff, Mr. Snell. I love my stuff. And I don't mind when it goes. Those are two different things. And I'll show you. I'll show you the difference. And you'll see. You'll see that the love stays."

And the hill pulled, and the hill pulled, and the hill pulled, and the house was huge, and the hum was loud, and the Keeper was keeping everything, and it did not know when to stop.

"Then we go in," said Pip. "We go in. We go to the heart. We go to the Keeper. And we answer the heartbreak. And we make it stop." He looked at the Bureau, at the walrus and the cat and the hedgehog and the girl and the six-year-old, and he looked at Snell, at the man shaped like an umbrella, and he said, "Are you ready?"

"I'm ready," said Barnaby.

"I'm ready," said Miss Whisk.

"I'm ready," said Thimble.

"I'm ready," said Goldie.

"I'm ready," said Ollie, at full volume, which was his way of signing a contract.

And Snell, who was a man shaped like an umbrella, who had been alone eight years, who had built a machine so nothing he loves is ever lost again, looked at the boy with the label-maker eyes, and he said, "I'm... I'm ready. I'm ready to... to try. I'm ready to try to let go." And the crack was there, wide, wider than it had ever been, and the man was ready to try, and the trying was the thing, and the trying was the start.

And they went to the door, the front door, the door of the house with too many curtains, and the door was open, because the Keeper was pulling, and the Keeper was pulling everything, and the door was a thing, and the door was being pulled, and the door was open. And they went in, into the house, into the keeping room, into the rooms that the outside of the house did not have room for, and the Keeper hummed, and the Keeper pulled, and the Keeper was keeping everything, and it did not know when to stop.

And the hill had grown teeth, metaphorically, and the teeth were the pull, and the pull was the Keeper, and the Keeper was keeping everything, and the Keeper did not know when to stop.

But the Bureau was in the house. The Bureau was in the house, and the Bureau was going to answer the heartbreak, and the Bureau was going to show the machine the love stays.

And the machine was going to find out. The machine was going to find out that the love stays, even when the thing is kept, even when the thing is gone.

But that was later. That was the climax. That was the answer.

For now, there was a hill with teeth, and a house that was huge, and a man who was ready to try to let go, and a pocket full of ready things.

For now, that was enough.
