## Chapter 15 — The Things That Were Loved Best

On the fifth day, the number changed, and Ollie, who was the Official Counter, was the one who noticed, because Official Counters notice when the number changes.

"Eighteen," he said. He was standing in front of the shelf with his pencil and his page, and his face was doing the thing it did when a number was wrong, which was the same thing it did when a dinosaur was lost. "It was seventeen. I counted it four times. It was seventeen. And now it's eighteen. And I didn't even count a new one. It just… became eighteen."

Pip came to the shelf. There was a new clean square in the dust. A new tag. *One harmonica. Plays a song that sounds like a train. Belonged to a man who played it to his plants. Claimed. Ready. Going home on Finding Day.* And no harmonica.

"It took another one," said Pip.

"It took another one," Miss Whisk agreed. She was already marking the ledger, already making the small precise mark in the margin, and her paw was steady but her ears were flat. "In the night. While we were all here. While the door was shut. While the rule was the rule." She looked at the great door at the back of the Bureau, the one that none of them had ever seen open. "The door is the only way in or out. The door was shut. And yet."

"And yet," Barnaby agreed, heavily. He had moved Captain Chomp in the night, to a shelf right behind his own desk, where he could see him, where his flipper could rest on the dinosaur while he worked. Captain Chomp sat there now, being dusted, being waited with, his loose button catching the light. He was safe. He was as safe as a thing could be in a room where safe was not a thing you could promise.

Pip looked at the eighteen clean squares, and he looked at the shelves around them, and he felt the label-maker feeling, the *there is a pattern here and I will find it* feeling. He walked the aisle. He looked at the things that were gone, and he looked at the things that were still there, and he compared them, the way you compare two lists to find the thing that is on one and not the other.

And then he saw it.

"Miss Whisk," he said. "The things that are gone. They're all… claimed."

Miss Whisk looked up. "What?"

"The things that are gone. The music box. The telescope. The harmonica. All the eighteen. They're all claimed. They're all ready. They're all going home on Finding Day." He pointed down the aisle. "But the things that are still here — the ones that aren't claimed, the ones that are just… waiting, the ones nobody's come for — they're all still here. Every one. The Keeper didn't take a single unclaimed thing."

The silence that followed was the kind where a pattern clicks into place, and the click is loud.

Miss Whisk turned the ledger. She checked. She checked the eighteen, and she checked the unclaimed, and she checked four times, because she was Miss Whisk and the truth was not wrong. And then she set the ledger down, and she said, in a voice that was very flat and very precise, "He's right. The Keeper takes the claimed. The ready. The ones that are loved back. The ones that remember the way home." She looked at Pip over her spectacles. "It does not take the unloved. It does not take the forgotten. It takes the things that are loved best."

"Why?" said Goldie. "Why only the loved ones?"

Barnaby was quiet for a long moment. His whiskers moved. His flipper rested on Captain Chomp, and the dinosaur's loose button caught the light.

"Because," he said, slowly, "the Keeper is a machine that keeps. And a machine that keeps does not want the things nobody wants. It wants the things that are wanted. It wants the love. It wants the… " He searched for the word. "The warmth. A thing that is loved is warm. A thing that is claimed is warm. And the Keeper is cold, and it is trying to be warm, and it is taking the warm things and putting them inside itself, and it thinks that will make it warm." He looked at the eighteen clean squares. "It is not stealing things. It is stealing the loving. It is taking the things that are loved best, because it wants to be loved best, and it does not know that you cannot get loved by keeping. You can only get loved by letting go."

The silence that followed had a temperature, and the temperature was the temperature of a machine that was cold and trying to be warm, and of a man who was lonely and trying to be not-lonely, and of eighteen things that were warm and were now kept.

Pip felt the ground under the fog go cold, because he understood, now, the thing that made the Keeper dangerous, and it was not that it was cruel. It was that it was hungry. It was hungry for the exact thing that Captain Chomp was full of. Captain Chomp, who was loved back, who was claimed, who was remembering the way home, who was warm. Captain Chomp was the warmest thing in the Bureau. And the Keeper took the warm things.

"Barnaby," said Pip. "Captain Chomp. He's… he's the warmest. He's claimed. He's ready. He's almost remembering. He's exactly what the Keeper takes."

Barnaby's flipper tightened on the dinosaur, gently, the way you hold a thing you are afraid of losing. "I know," he said. "That is why he is behind my desk. That is why I do not leave him. That is why I am the Chief, and why I have been the Chief for a very long time, and why I will be the Chief until this is answered." He looked at Pip with his small, kind eyes. "The Keeper will come for him. It will come for the warmest thing. And when it does, I will be here. And I will say no. And we will see whether a machine that keeps things can keep a thing that a walrus is holding."

It was a brave thing to say, and it was a sad thing to say, and Pip loved Barnaby for saying it, and he was also, for the first time, a little afraid for Barnaby, because a walrus is very large and very kind, but a machine that does not know when to stop is a machine that does not know when to stop, and kindness is not a thing you can hold against a gear.

"We have to answer it first," said Pip. "Before it comes for Chomp. Before Finding Day. We have to teach it to stop."

"Yes," said Barnaby. "But we do not yet know how. And the machine is getting stronger. It took a thing in the night, while the door was shut. It is reaching further. It is… " He paused, and he looked at the case board, at the gear under the WHY. "It is missing a gear. And a machine that is missing a gear reaches harder. It compensates. It pulls. It is pulling harder than it was."

Pip felt the thing he had been afraid of settle into him. The gear. The gear they had taken. The Keeper had noticed its gear was missing, and it was reaching harder, and it was taking more, and it was going to take the warmest thing, and the warmest thing was a small green dinosaur who roared in his sleep.

"Three days," said Pip. "Finding Day is in three days. We have three days to answer a heartbreak and teach a machine to stop."

"Three days," Barnaby agreed. "And a case board. And a gear. And a why. And a six-year-old who is very good at counting."

Ollie, who was the Official Counter, looked up from his page. "Eighteen," he said. "I'm going to keep counting. And when it goes back down — when the things come home — I'm going to count them down. And I'm going to count all the way to zero. And zero is the best number. Zero means nothing's missing." He looked at Pip with his whole face. "I'm going to count to zero, Pip. I promise. That's my job. I'm the Official Counter. I count to zero."

And Pip, who was eleven, who had a label maker, who had a brother who was very good at counting, felt the ground under the fog go solid, because a room where a six-year-old promises to count to zero is a room that has decided what winning looks like.

Zero. Nothing missing. Everything home.

"Okay," said Pip. "Okay, Ollie. You count to zero. And I'll find the how." He looked at the case board, at the WHY, at the gear, at the string. "Tomorrow we watch the Keeper. Tomorrow we see it take something. And tomorrow we find the crack in it. Because every machine has a crack. Every cage has a door. And every heartbreak…" He thought of Goldie's pocket, of the marble and the paperclip and the rubber band, of *the sad is the good kind, if you let it be.* "Every heartbreak has an answer. We just have to find it."

Under the hill, the Keeper hummed, patient and keeping, reaching harder, pulling warmer, and it had eighteen things, and it wanted the nineteenth, and the nineteenth was a small green dinosaur behind a walrus's desk.

But that was tomorrow's problem. Tonight there was a number that was going to go back down, and an Official Counter who was going to count it, and a boy who had decided to find the crack.

Tonight, that was enough.
