## Chapter 23 - The Dog-Shaped Hole

On the seventh day, they went back, and Snell made tea, and he made the biscuits, the dog kind, and he set them on a plate, and he looked at the plate for a long moment before he set it down, and Pip saw the look, and filed it, and did not say anything.

They sat in the keeping room, among the shelves and the kept things, and the Keeper hummed under the floor, and Snell folded his long hands, and he said, "You asked about the dog. About Biscuit. I... I would like to tell you. If you'd like to hear. Nobody's... nobody's asked about Biscuit in a long time. And I think... I think I would like to say his name out loud. To someone. I've been saying it to the Keeper. And the Keeper hums. But the Keeper doesn't... the Keeper doesn't remember him. Not the way I remember him. And I think I would like someone to remember him with me."

"We'd like to hear," said Pip.

And Snell told them.

"Biscuit was a stray," he said. "Did you know that? He wasn't a dog I bought. He was a dog that came. I was... I was alone, then. I've always been alone, mostly. I'm a maker. I make things. And makers are alone, mostly, because the things don't talk back. And I was alone, and I left the bowl out. Just... just a bowl. By the door. For strays. For the strays that come in the night. And one night, a dog came. A biscuit-colored dog. Thin, then. Not fat. Thin, and wary, and hungry. And he drank from the bowl. And he looked at me. And I... I left the bowl out the next night. And he came back. And I left it out the next. And he came back. And after a while, he stopped being a stray. He stopped being a stray, and he started being Biscuit. And I... I named him Biscuit, because he was the color of a biscuit, and because he was the only thing in my life that was never once late."

Snell paused, and he looked at the plate of dog biscuits, and his face was the face of a man looking at a thing he has lost and has not stopped loving.

"He was a good dog," he said. "He was a very good dog. He used to sit by the oven while I made things. He used to... he used to put his head on my foot when I worked. Just his head. On my foot. And I would work, and he would sleep, and his head would be on my foot, and I... I was not alone. For the first time in a long time, I was not alone. Because there was a dog, and his head was on my foot, and he was never once late."

The silence that followed had a temperature, and the temperature was a dog's head on a man's foot, and a man who was not alone for the first time in a long time.

"And then," said Snell, and his voice went soft, "and then he ran off."

"He... he chased something," said Snell. "A rabbit. Or a sound. Or a... I don't know. I don't know what he chased. I was in the workshop. I was making something. And I heard the gate, and I heard the... the running, and I came out, and he was gone. Down the lane. And I called. And I called. And he didn't come back." Snell's long hands folded tighter. "He didn't come back. And I looked. I looked for a long time. I put up posters. I put up... I put up posters with his picture. And the posters got rained on. And I put up more. And they got rained on. And I... I took them down. Eventually. I took them down. And I stopped coming in to the bakery. And I closed the curtains. And I... I built the Keeper."

"Because you couldn't bear it," said Pip.

"Because I couldn't bear it," Snell agreed. "Because I couldn't bear the thought of a thing I love being lost. Being gone. Being... being out there, somewhere, cold, and hungry, and wondering why I didn't come. And I thought... I thought, if I could build a thing that keeps, a thing that never loses, then I would never have to feel this again. This... " He put his hand on his chest, over his heart, and he said, "This hole. This dog-shaped hole. I thought, if I keep everything, I'll never have another hole. I'll never have another... another dog-shaped hole." He looked at Pip with his kind, tired eyes. "But the hole is still there. The Keeper keeps everything, and the hole is still there. Because the hole is not... the hole is not about the things. The hole is about Biscuit. And the Keeper can't keep Biscuit. Because Biscuit is gone. And the Keeper can keep everything else, but it can't keep Biscuit. And the hole is still there."

Pip felt the ground under the fog go warm and sad. The man knew. The man knew, somewhere, under the keeping, under the dusting, under the telling-things-they're-loved, the man knew that the Keeper could not fill the hole. The man knew the hole was about Biscuit, and that keeping everything else would not bring him back. And he kept anyway. Because keeping was the thing he could do, and the hole was the thing he could not fix, and keeping was the only thing left that looked like love.

Ollie, who had been listening with his whole face, who had been quiet for an unusually long time, said, "Mr. Snell. I lost Captain Chomp. My dinosaur. He's a secret pirate. And he was under my chin, and then he wasn't. And I... I know the hole. I know the dog-shaped hole. I have a dinosaur-shaped hole. And it's... it's the worst. It's the worst hole." He looked at Snell with his whole face. "And I'm getting him back. On Finding Day. He's being loved back. He's remembering the way home. And I'm going to get him back." He paused, and he said, the bravest thing, the small, loud, brave thing, "And maybe... maybe Biscuit is being loved back too. Maybe Biscuit is remembering the way home. Maybe Biscuit is... maybe Biscuit is not gone. Maybe Biscuit is just lost. And lost is not the same as gone."

Snell looked at the six-year-old who had just said *lost is not the same as gone*, and the crack opened wider than it had ever been. He put his long hands over his face and made a sound that was not quite a sob and not quite a hum - eight years of keeping, coming loose.

Goldie reached out and put her hand on Snell's arm - a thing you did to a bell, not to a man shaped like an umbrella - and said, "Lost is not the same as gone, Mr. Snell. Lost is not the same as gone. And the love stays. Even when the thing is lost. The love stays. That's the thing. That's the whole thing. The love stays."

And Snell, under his long hands, said, "The love stays," soft and broken and wondering, the way you say a thing you have been avoiding for eight years and have just been told is true.

The love stays.

Under the floor, the Keeper hummed. And for a second, just a second, the hum did something it had never done. It faltered. It skipped. It... it missed a beat, the way a heart misses a beat when it hears a thing it has been avoiding. And then it caught, and it went on, patient and keeping, but the beat had been missed, and Pip heard it, and filed it, and put a little star on the tag.

The Keeper had heard. The Keeper had heard *the love stays*, and the Keeper had missed a beat. And a machine that misses a beat is a machine that has a crack. And a crack is where the light gets in. And a crack is where the answer starts.

They stayed a little longer. They finished their tea. They ate the biscuits, the dog kind, because Snell had made them, and because you did not leave biscuits unfinished in a house that had made them, and because the biscuits were the only thing left that looked like love. And Snell, when they left, did not say *thank you for coming*. He said, "The love stays," softly, to himself, the way you say a thing you are trying to believe. And the door closed, gently, the way he did everything.

And they stood in the lane, the three of them, and the house with too many curtains held its breath, and the Keeper hummed, patient and keeping, but the beat had been missed, and the crack was there, and the love was still there, even after eight years, even with the curtains closed.

And Pip, who was eleven, who had a label maker, who had just watched a six-year-old ring a bell with the words *lost is not the same as gone*, felt the shape of the answer settle into him, hard and clear. They were going to answer the heartbreak. They were going to show the man the love stays. They were going to get nineteen things home for Finding Day, and one dinosaur, and one dog-shaped hole.

They were going to do it tomorrow. On Finding Day. With a pocket full of useless junk, and a cat with a stamp, and a walrus who says no, and a six-year-old who counts to zero.

But tonight, there was a man saying *the love stays* to himself, softly, the way you say a thing you are trying to believe.

And tonight, that was a start.
