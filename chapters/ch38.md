## Chapter 38 — The Keeping Lever

The things had gone home, and the Keeper was quiet, and the quiet was a thing that had a sound, and the sound was the sound of a machine that has let go of everything except the one thing it was built around.

Because the Keeper was still humming. Faintly. At the edge of things. A low, steady, keeping note, but small now, small and tired, the hum of a machine that has let go of everything except the first thing, the thing in the jar, the collar tag, the dog, the love. And the Keeper was still holding the collar tag. And the Keeper was still built around the collar tag. And the Keeper was not going to stop, not all the way, not until the collar tag was let go. And the collar tag was the thing Snell had to let go. And the letting go was the thing Snell had to do. And the doing was the thing that was hard.

"There's a lever," said Snell. He was standing at the heart of the house, at the Keeper, at the brass and the gears and the springs and the little copper wires, and he was shaped like an umbrella, and his long hands were folded, and his face was the face of a man who is about to do the thing he has been avoiding for eight years. "There's a lever. The keeping lever. The… the release. I built it. I built it so… so I could stop. In case. In case I ever… in case I ever wanted to stop. And I never… I never pulled it. I never pulled it. Because pulling it means… pulling it means letting go. And I… I don't know how to let go. I've been keeping for eight years. And I don't know how to let go."

And the lever was there. The keeping lever. The release. A small brass lever, set into the heart of the machine, next to the jar, next to the collar tag, next to the first thing. And the lever was the thing that would stop the Keeper. And the lever was the thing that would let go. And the lever was the thing Snell had to pull. And the pulling was the thing that was hard.

"Mr. Snell," said Pip. "The love stays. Even when the thing is gone. The love stays. You don't have to keep the collar tag to keep the love. The love is not in the collar tag. The love is in you. And the love stays. Even when the collar tag is let go. Even when Biscuit is… even when Biscuit is gone. The love stays."

"But Biscuit is gone," said Snell, and his voice broke on the word *gone*, the way a voice breaks when a man has been keeping everything for eight years and has just decided to let one thing go. "Biscuit is gone. And the collar tag is… the collar tag is the last thing. The collar tag is the last thing I have of him. And if I let it go… if I let it go, then… then he's really gone. Then he's really gone. And I can't… I can't let him be really gone. I can't."

And the silence that followed had a temperature, and the temperature was a collar tag, and a dog that was gone, and a man who could not let the last thing go.

And then Ollie, who was six, who was brave in small, loud ways, said, "Mr. Snell. Biscuit's not gone. Biscuit's lost. And lost is not the same as gone. And… and maybe Biscuit is being loved back. Maybe Biscuit is remembering the way home. Maybe Biscuit is… maybe Biscuit is not gone. Maybe Biscuit is just lost. And lost is not the same as gone." He looked at Snell with his whole face. "And the love stays. Even when the thing is lost. The love stays. And… and maybe the thing comes back. Maybe the thing comes back differently. But the love stays. And maybe… maybe Biscuit comes back. Maybe Biscuit comes back differently. But the love stays."

And Snell, who was a man shaped like an umbrella, who had been alone eight years, looked at the six-year-old who had just said *maybe Biscuit comes back differently*, and the crack opened, wider than it had ever been, and for a long moment he did not speak. And then he said, "Differently. Biscuit comes back… differently." He looked at the collar tag, at the small metal thing, at the first thing, at the thing that started it. "Differently. He'd be… he'd be old now. He'd be fat. He'd be… he'd be different. He'd be different. And I… I would still love him. I would still love him. Even different. Even old. Even fat. I would still love him. Because the love stays. The love stays. Even different. Even when the thing comes back differently. The love stays."

And he reached into the jar. He reached into the heart of the machine, and he took out the collar tag, the small metal thing, the first thing, the thing that started it, and he held it in his long hand, and he looked at it, and he said, "Biscuit. Biscuit. I… I loved you. I loved you. And I kept everything. I kept everything so I would never lose anything again. And I… I lost the loving. I lost the loving. Because keeping is not loving. Keeping is being afraid, with a machine. And I was afraid. And I was afraid for eight years. And I… I'm not going to be afraid anymore. I'm not going to be afraid anymore. I'm going to let go. I'm going to let go. And the love stays. The love stays. Even when the thing is gone. Even when the thing comes back differently. The love stays."

And he put the collar tag in his pocket. Not in the machine. Not in the jar. Not in the keeping. In his pocket. A healthy keeping. A carrying. A leaving-the-bowl-out. And he pulled the lever.

And the Keeper stopped.

The Keeper stopped the way a thing stops when a man has let go, which is to say with a sound, which is to say with a sigh, which is to say with a long, low, mechanical sigh, the sound of a machine that has been keeping for eight years and has just been told that the love stays. And the hum went out. The hum went out like a candle. And the brass went still. And the gears went still. And the springs went still. And the little copper wires went still. And the Keeper was not a Keeper anymore. The Keeper was a machine. The Keeper was a thing. The Keeper was a… the Keeper was a shed. The Keeper was an ordinary shed. The Keeper was a thing that had been keeping and had just let go. And the house stopped growing. The house stopped growing, and the rooms… the rooms did not go away, but the rooms stopped growing, and the house was a house again, and the house was not a Bureau that keeps, and the house was a house, and the house was a home.

And the silence that followed was the silence of a machine that has let go, and the silence was loud, and the silence was the loudest thing, because the hum was gone, and the hum had been there for eight years, and the hum was gone, and the gone was a thing you could hear. And the gone was a thing that was a relief. And the gone was a thing that was a start.

And Snell, who was a man shaped like an umbrella, who had been alone eight years, who had built a machine so nothing he loves is ever lost again, who had just let go, stood at the heart of the house, at the ordinary shed, at the thing that had been a Keeper, and he held the collar tag in his pocket, and he said, "The love stays. The love stays. I… I let go. And the love stays. I let go. And the love stays." And his face was the face of a man who has just done the thing he has been avoiding for eight years, and the thing is true, and the thing is a relief, and the thing is a start.

The collar tag was in his pocket, and it was warm, and it was a homing thing. It wanted Biscuit. It wanted the dog. It wanted home. And it led the way.

The sock trail was still there, leading from the house, through the green door, down the hill, into the town. Toward the bakery. Toward The Warm Loaf. Toward the bowl. Toward the dog.

The collar tag followed the sock trail, and Snell followed the collar tag, toward the bakery, toward the bowl, toward the dog.

"Mr. Snell," said Pip. "The collar tag. The collar tag wants home. The collar tag wants Biscuit. The collar tag… the collar tag is leading the way. Follow the collar tag. Follow the sock trail. Follow the way home."

And Snell, who was a man shaped like an umbrella, who had been alone eight years, who had just let go, followed the collar tag, and the collar tag followed the sock trail, and the sock trail led down the hill, into the town, toward the bakery, toward The Warm Loaf, toward the bowl, toward the dog.

And the reunion was coming. The reunion was coming. The reunion was the next chapter. The reunion was the answer.

But that was later. That was the reunion. That was the next chapter.

For now, there was a man who had let go, and a collar tag in a pocket, and a sock trail that led home, and a Keeper that was an ordinary shed.

For now, that was enough.
