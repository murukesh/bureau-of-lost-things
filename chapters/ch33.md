## Chapter 33 — A Plan with Socks in It

They made the plan on the back of a lost door, because the case board was there, and because Goldie said the best plans were made on lost things, because lost things knew what it was to need a plan.

"Here's what we've got," said Pip, printing labels and sticking them to the door, connecting them with string Goldie found in her pocket. "The pocket. The button. The label. The socks. And the love stays, which is the answer. The rest is the how. So let's figure out the how."

"The pocket first," said Goldie, and she turned the pocket half inside out, the way she did, and let the contents spill into her palm. "Exhibit A. The marble. The paperclip. The rubber band. Three useless things. Three ready things." She held up the marble, the cat's-eye, the swirl of green and gold. "The marble. It's glass. It's not brass. It's not metal. And the Keeper… the Keeper is brass. The Keeper pulls with the brass — the metal things, the things that answer to the brass. But the marble is glass. Glass does not answer to the brass. Glass does not get pulled." She set the marble on the door, and it rolled an inch, and she stopped it with one finger without looking. "So the marble can go where we can't. Into the Keeper. It can roll to the heart and show us the off."

"The off?" said Ollie.

"The off," said Goldie. "Every machine has an off. Every machine has a… a place where it stops. And the marble is going to find it. The marble is going to roll into the Keeper, and the marble is going to roll to the heart, and the marble is going to show us the off. Because the marble is glass. And the glass does not get pulled. And the glass can go where we can't." She looked at Pip. "That's the marble's job. It's been useless for six years so it could be ready for this one."

And Pip, who was eleven, who had a label maker, who had just heard a girl say *the marble's been useless for six years so it could be ready for this job*, felt the thing click, quietly, in the drawer where he kept the things that mattered. He filed it. He filed it with a little star on the tag. *The marble finds the off.*

"The paperclip," said Goldie, and she held up the paperclip, silver and bent. "The paperclip is bent. The paperclip is thin. And the paperclip can pick. The paperclip can pick a latch. The paperclip can pick a panel. The paperclip can open the thing that needs opening. The Keeper has a panel. The Keeper has a… a maintenance panel. A place where you get in. And the panel is latched. And the paperclip can pick the latch. And the paperclip can open the panel. And then we can get to the heart. And then we can get to the off." She hooked the paperclip onto her collar. "That's the paperclip's job. Bent for years, so it's ready for this one."

"The rubber band," said Goldie, and she held up the rubber band, red and cracked and stretched. "The rubber band is… the rubber band is a snap. The rubber band is a… a thing that snaps. And the Keeper has a belt. The Keeper has a… a drive belt. A thing that turns. And the rubber band can snap the belt. Or… or the rubber band can launch. The rubber band can launch the first sock. The rubber band can… can start the trail." She looked at Thimble. "That's the rubber band's job. But it needs the socks. And the socks are Thimble's. And the socks are the homing things. And the socks are the trail."

And Thimble, who sorted the socks, who gave spare buttons to lost coats, who was prepared for loss, said, "The socks. The lonely socks. They want home. They want home more than anything. More than a glove wants its glove. More than a key wants its lock. A lonely sock is a homing thing. And a homing thing does not stay kept. A homing thing finds its way." He looked at the bin, the special bin, the one he had labeled READY / GOING HOME / SMELLS LIKE SOMEONE, and he said, "When the Keeper stops… when the Keeper is jammed, when the Keeper is… when the Keeper is not pulling… the socks go home. The socks find their pairs. The socks find their people. And the socks lead the way. The socks lead the way home. And the things follow the socks home. That's the thing about lonely socks. They're homing things. And a homing thing leads the way."

"And the button," said Pip, and he looked at Thimble, and Thimble's quills clicked, and the buttons were there, the spare buttons, the promises. "The button. The button jams the gears. The button is… the button is a thing that jams. A button in the gears. A button in the… the teeth. And the gears stop. And the Keeper is jammed. And then the socks go home. And then the things follow. And then the love stays." He looked at Thimble. "That's the button's job. The spare is a promise. And the promise is… the promise is I expect you to come loose, and I expect to fix you, and I am not going anywhere. And the button is going to fix the Keeper. The button is going to fix the Keeper by… by jamming it. By making it stop. By making it… by making it let go."

And Thimble, who was small and round and the color of toast, who had given a red star button to a lost coat, who had had the red star button popped off and taken, who was going to have a word, said, "The button. Yes. The button. I have a great many spares. I am prepared for loss. And I am going to use it to jam the gears. And I am going to have a word. And the word is let go." And his quills clicked, and the buttons were there, and he was going to use one, and he was going to have a word.

"And the label," said Pip, and he took out the label maker, and he printed a label, and the label said FOUND. COMES BACK TO., and it was the label he had pressed onto the green door, the crooked one, the one that was slightly crooked, the one that would matter. "The label. The label marks the door. The green door. The door is… the door is going to move. The Keeper is going to pull the door. The Keeper is going to pull everything, and the door is a thing, and the door is going to move. And when the door moves, the label marks it. And we can find it. And we can follow it. And the label is the way home." He looked at the label, at the crooked printing, and he said, "The label's a promise. The label says I expect you to come loose, and I expect to fix you, and I am not going anywhere. And the label is going to mark the door, and lead us back, and be the way home."

The plan was the marble finds the off, the paperclip opens the panel, the button jams the gears, the rubber band launches the first sock, the socks lead the way home, the label marks the door. It was absurd. It was a pocket full of junk and a spare button and a crooked label and a bin of lonely socks. It was the love stays. It was the answer.

"It's a terrible plan," said Goldie, grinning the shortcut grin. "The worst. A plan made of junk and socks and a button. A plan that should not work." She patted the pocket, and the marble and the paperclip and the rubber band clicked together. "And it's going to work, because it's made of things that are loved. And things that are loved are ready. And things that are ready save the day. That's the thing about useless things. They're useless so they're ready."

Pip felt the thing settle into him, hard and clear, like a label pressed onto a thing that was not going anywhere. The plan was the answer. The plan was the love stays. And the plan was going to work, because it was made of the things that are loved.

"Then we go," said Pip. "Up the hill. To the Keeper. And we answer the heartbreak. And we make it stop."

They were ready. All of them. Even Ollie, who was going to count to zero, who had promised, who was the Official Counter.

And they went up the hill, toward the house with too many curtains, toward the Keeper, toward the machine that had learned to want. The plan was a pocket full of junk, a spare button, a crooked label, and a bin of lonely socks. The plan was the love stays. And the plan was going to work.
