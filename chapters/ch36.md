## Chapter 36 - A Button, a Paperclip, and a Very Brave Label

The Keeper gave one last pull, and the pull was a big one, and the pull was a thing that reached out of the house and down the hill and into the town, and the pull grabbed the green door.

The green door. The small green door behind the school's lost-and-found bin. The door that was shy. The door that only showed itself to people who had lost something they truly loved. The door that was the way in, and the way out, and the way home. The Keeper grabbed it, and the Keeper pulled it, and the green door came out of the school wall with a sound like a tooth coming out of a gum, and it flew up the hill, and it landed in the keeping room, and it lay there, on the floor, among the kept things, and it was being kept, and the way home was being kept.

"The door," said Pip. "The door. The way home. It's... it's being kept. The Keeper took the door. The Keeper took the way home." He looked at the green door, on the floor, among the kept things, and he felt the thing go cold, because the way home was being kept, and the things could not go home if the way home was being kept, and the Keeper was keeping the way home.

And then he saw the label.

The label. The crooked one. The one he had pressed onto the green door on the third day, the one that said FOUND. COMES BACK TO., the one that was slightly crooked, the one that he had pressed onto the door because he wanted the door to know it was found. The label was on the green door. The label was crooked. And the label was the thing that marked the door, the thing that said *this is the door, this is the way home, this is the thing that comes back to*, and the label was the thing that made the door not just a kept thing, but the way home.

"The label," said Pip. "The label marks the door. The label marks the way home." He went to the green door, and he knelt down, and he pressed the label flat, the crooked label, the FOUND. COMES BACK TO., and he said, "You're not kept. You're the way home. You're the way home. You come back to. You come back to." And he propped the green door up, against the wall, against the keeping, and he opened it, and the door opened, and behind the door was not the keeping room, behind the door was the schoolyard, behind the door was the town, behind the door was the way home. And the way home was open. And the way home was marked. And the way home was found.

And the Keeper pulled, and the Keeper pulled, and the Keeper tried to close the door, and the door would not close, because the label was on it, because the label said FOUND. COMES BACK TO., because the label was a promise, because the label said I expect you to come loose, and I expect to fix you, and I am not going anywhere. And the door would not close. And the way home was open. And the way home was found.

"The panel," said Goldie. "The panel. The maintenance panel. The place where you get in. The place where the gears are. The place where the button goes." She was looking at the Keeper, at the brass, at the gears, at the springs, at the little copper wires, and she was looking at the panel, the maintenance panel, the place where you get in, and the panel was latched, and the latch was brass, and the latch was pulled, and the latch was being kept. "The latch is brass. The latch is being kept. I can't... I can't open it with my hands. The latch is being kept."

"The paperclip," said Pip. "The paperclip. The paperclip picks the latch. The paperclip opens the panel."

And Goldie took out the paperclip. The silver one. The bent one. The one that was bent because she had bent it thinking about her dad. The one that had been bent for years so it could be ready for this job. And she bent it, a little, a little more, the way you bend a paperclip when you are thinking, and she slid it into the latch, and she picked, and she picked, and she picked, and the latch was brass, and the latch was being kept, but the paperclip was thin, and the paperclip was bent, and the paperclip was ready, and the paperclip picked the latch, and the latch opened, and the panel opened, and the gears were there.

"The gears," said Thimble. "The gears. The teeth. The place where the button goes." And he reached up, very carefully, and he worked a button loose from his quills. A small brown button. Four-holed. The kind that holds a coat together. The kind that is a promise. The kind that says I expect you to come loose, and I expect to fix you, and I am not going anywhere. And he held the button in his paw, and he looked at the gears, at the teeth, at the turning, and he said, "The spare is a promise. And the promise is... the promise is let go. And I'm going to fix you. I'm going to fix you by making you stop. I'm going to fix you by making you let go." And he put the button in the gears.

And the gears stopped.

The button went into the teeth, and the teeth caught the button, and the button was a thing that did not turn, and the gears were a thing that turned, and the thing that did not turn met the thing that turned, and the thing that turned stopped. And the Keeper stopped. The Keeper stopped the way a thing stops when a button is in its gears, which is to say with a sound, which is to say with a grind, which is to say with a whine, a small, mechanical whine, the sound of a machine that has been told to stop by a hedgehog's spare button. And the Keeper stopped. And the pull stopped. And the wanting stopped. And the not-thinking stopped. And the hill let go. And the town let go. And the coats let go. And the buttons let go. And the socks let go. And the umbrellas let go. And the Keeper stopped.

And the silence that followed was the silence of a machine that has been told to stop, and the silence was loud, and the silence was the loudest thing, because the hum was gone, and the hum had been there for eight years, and the hum was gone, and the gone was a thing you could hear.

"It stopped," said Ollie, and his voice was small, and his voice was loud, and his voice was the voice of a six-year-old who is watching the number stop going the wrong way. "It stopped. The Keeper stopped. The number stopped going up. The number stopped going up." He looked at his page, at the OFFICIAL COUNTER, at the number, and he said, "Now I count down. Now I count down. Now I count to zero. Now I count to zero."

And the button had jammed the gears. And the paperclip had opened the panel. And the label had marked the door. And the three things had done the job. And the three things were useless. And the three things were ready. And the three things had been useless so they could be ready. And the three things had saved the day.

And Thimble, who was small and round and the color of toast, who had given a red star button to a lost coat, who had had the red star button popped off and taken, who had just put a small brown button in the gears of a machine that keeps, said, "There. There. I fixed you. I fixed you. I expect you to come loose, and I expect to fix you, and I am not going anywhere. And I fixed you. And I'm not going anywhere. And the word is let go. And the word is let go." And his quills clicked, and the buttons were there, one fewer, and the one fewer was the one that had done the job, and the one that had done the job was the one that was a promise, and the promise was let go.

And the Keeper was stopped. And the Keeper was jammed. And the Keeper was not pulling. And the Keeper was not keeping. And the way home was open. And the way home was marked. And the way home was found.

But the Keeper was not let go. The Keeper was stopped, but the Keeper was not let go. The Keeper was jammed, but the Keeper was still keeping. The Keeper was still holding the things. The Keeper was still holding the eighteen, and the nineteen, and the twenty, and the everything. And the things were still kept. And the things were still cold. And the things were still almost.

"The socks," said Thimble. "The socks. The lonely socks. The socks go home. The socks lead the way. The socks lead the way home. And the things follow. And the things follow the socks home. And the Keeper... the Keeper lets go. The Keeper lets go when the socks go home. The Keeper lets go when the love stays."

And the next thing was the socks. And the next thing was the rubber band. And the next thing was the trail. And the next thing was the home.

But that was later. That was the next chapter. That was the answer.

For now, there was a button in the gears, and a paperclip in the latch, and a label on the door, and a Keeper that was stopped.

For now, that was enough.
