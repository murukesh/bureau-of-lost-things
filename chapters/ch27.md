## Chapter 27 — One Dinosaur, Express Delivery

Snell came to the Bureau to let the dinosaur go, and he came through the big door, the one at the back, the one that was the only way in or out, and he came quietly, the way he did everything, and he was shaped like an umbrella, and he was carrying a plate of biscuits, the dog kind, because you did not let a dinosaur go without biscuits, because it was a rule, he had just made it up, but it was true.

Barnaby met him at the big door. The walrus and the man shaped like an umbrella stood there, in the Bureau, among the shelves and the waiting things, and the Keeper hummed under the hill, and for a long moment neither of them spoke. And then Barnaby said, "Silas. It's been a long time."

"It has," said Snell. "It's been eight years. I… I didn't come. I didn't come in. I was… I was building. I was building the Keeper. And I didn't come in." He looked at the Bureau, at the waiting things, at the umbrella orchard and the jar of humming keys, and his face was the face of a man looking at a thing he used to know. "I used to come in. Before. I used to bring the… I used to bring the lost things. I used to help. Did you know that? I used to help. I was a Friend of the Lost. Before. Before the dog. Before the Keeper. I was a Friend of the Lost."

"I know," said Barnaby, softly. "I made up the title. You were one of the first. You were… you were very good at it. You were very good at the waiting. You were very good at the loving back." He paused, and his small eyes were very gentle. "You still are, Silas. You still are. You've just… you've just forgotten the difference between the waiting and the keeping. And I think… I think you're starting to remember."

And Snell, who was a man shaped like an umbrella, who had been alone eight years, looked at the walrus who had made up the title, and the crack opened, wider than it had ever been, and for a long moment he did not speak. And then he said, "The dinosaur. I came to let the dinosaur go. That's the deal. That's the deal. One dinosaur. Let go home. Before… before the Keeper keeps everything. I came to let the dinosaur go."

"I know," said Barnaby. "Come. He's behind my desk. He's been… he's been being dusted. He's been being waited with. He's been remembering the way home." He led Snell to the desk, and Captain Chomp was there, on his shelf, with his two mismatched buttons and his twice-sewn tail, and he was warm, he was the warmest thing, he was being loved back, and he was remembering.

And Snell, who was a man shaped like an umbrella, who had built a machine so nothing he loves is ever lost again, looked at the small green dinosaur, and he picked him up, gently, the way he did everything, and he held him, and he said, "There now. There now. You're… you're going home. You're going home. You're not… you're not going to be kept. You're going home." And his voice broke on the word *home*, the way a voice breaks when a man has been keeping everything for eight years and has just decided to let one thing go.

And the Keeper protested.

Under the hill, under the floor, the Keeper hummed, and the hum did something it had never done. It faltered. It skipped. It… it whined, a small, mechanical whine, the sound of a machine that has been told to let go and does not want to. And the shelves shook, just a little, just a tremor, and a button popped off a coat somewhere, and the umbrella orchard made its rain-that-wasn't-there sound, louder, and the jar of keys hummed its question, louder, and the Keeper whined, and it did not want to let go.

"Let him go," said Snell, to the Keeper, to the machine, to the heart of the house. "Let him go. He's… he's going home. He's going home. Let him go." And his voice was gentle, and it was firm, and it was the voice of a man who has decided to let one thing go, and the Keeper whined, and it faltered, and it… it let go. The hum went down, down, down, and the tremor stopped, and the shelves were still, and Captain Chomp was warm, and he was free, and he was going home.

And Miss Whisk, who kept the record, stamped the ledger, and the stamp said GOING HOME, and it was the first GOING HOME she had stamped all week, and her paw did not shake, and the almost-smile was there, in the corner, permanent now.

And Snell, who was a man shaped like an umbrella, who had just let one thing go, held the dinosaur out to Ollie, and he said, "Here. Here's the captain. Here's the… here's the secret pirate. He's going home. He's… he's not going to be kept. He's going home." And his long hands were shaking, just a little, just a tremor, the way a man's hands shake when he has let go of a thing he has been keeping, and Ollie took the dinosaur with both hands, the way you take a thing that is made of glass, that is made of gold, and he held him to his chin, and he said, "Captain Chomp. Captain Chomp. You're home. You're home. You're home."

And Captain Chomp's loose button caught the light, and his twice-sewn tail seemed to wag, just a little, just a tremor, and he was home, he was home, he was home.

And Snell, who was a man shaped like an umbrella, who had just let one thing go, watched the boy hold the dinosaur, and his face was the face of a man who has just remembered a thing he has been avoiding for eight years, which was that letting go is not the same as losing, and that the love stays, even when the thing goes home. And the crack was there, wide, wider than it had ever been, and for a long moment Pip thought the man was going to say *the love stays*. And then the man said, "Watch me," softly, to himself, the way you say a thing you are trying to believe, and the crack closed, just a little, just a crack, and the man turned to go.

"Mr. Snell," said Pip. "Thank you. For the dinosaur. Thank you. But we're not done. We're not done. We're going to show you. We're going to show you the love stays. And we're going to do it on Finding Day. And you're going to see."

"Watch me," said Snell, softly, and he went out the big door, quietly, the way he did everything, and the door closed behind him, and the Keeper hummed, patient and keeping, and it had let one thing go, and it did not like it, and it was going to keep everything else, and it was getting ready for Finding Day.

And Ollie, who was six, who was brave in small, loud ways, held Captain Chomp to his chin, and he said, "We're going home. We're going home. We're going home. And tomorrow is Finding Day. And tomorrow, everything's going home. And I'm going to count to zero. I'm going to count to zero. I promise."

And Pip, who was eleven, who had a label maker, who had just watched a man shaped like an umbrella let one thing go, felt the ground under the fog go warm and cold at the same time, because the man had let one thing go, and the man had said *watch me*, and the man was going to keep everything else, and the love was still there, even after eight years, even with the curtains closed, and they were going to prove it.

They were going to prove it. Tomorrow. On Finding Day. With a pocket full of useless junk, and a cat with a stamp, and a walrus who says no, and a hedgehog who sorts the socks, and a six-year-old who counts to zero.

But tonight, there was a dinosaur going home, and a man who had let one thing go, and a Keeper that did not like it, and a boy who had decided to answer a heartbreak.

And tonight, that was a start.
