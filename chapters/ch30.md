## Chapter 30 — The House Grew a New Room

They celebrated, because you celebrated a homecoming, because a homecoming was a thing you celebrated, because the captain was home and the label said HOME, COMES BACK TO, and the shoebox chair had been upgraded to a proper chair, which Ollie had made out of a shoebox and a great deal of tape and a great deal of love, and the celebration was a celebration.

Barnaby came to the narrow house, which was a thing he did not do, which was a thing he had never done, because the Chief of the Bureau did not leave the Bureau, except on Finding Day, except for a homecoming, except for a secret pirate who had been loved back and had remembered the way. He came through the green door, which showed itself to him because he had lost something he truly loved, which was the whole town, which was the whole waiting, and he came up the hill, and he came to the narrow house, and he was the size of a sofa and gray and could not, by any reasonable definition, be invisible, and he did not try to be, because a homecoming was a thing you were visible for.

"To the captain," said Barnaby, raising a bun, because he had brought buns, because you did not come to a homecoming without buns, because it was a rule, he had just made it up, but it was true. "To the captain. To the secret pirate. To the hat that is invisible. To the homecoming." He paused, and his whiskers moved, and he said, "To the first thing going home. To the first of the many. To the zero that is coming."

"To zero," said Ollie, at full volume, which was his way of signing a contract.

And Miss Whisk came, which was a thing she did not do, which was a thing she had never done, because the Head Cataloguer did not leave the record, except for a homecoming, except to stamp a GOING HOME in person, and she came, and she stamped the ledger, which she had brought, because you did not leave the record at home, and the stamp said GOING HOME, and her paw did not shake, and the almost-smile was there, in the corner, permanent now.

And Thimble came, which was a thing he did not do, because the rain ones were difficult, because the rain ones smelled like bus stops, but he came, because a homecoming was a thing you came to, and he brought a button, a green one, round, four-holed, almost exactly the green of Captain Chomp, and he said, "For the captain. A spare. In case. That's what spares are for." And Ollie took it with both hands, the way you take a thing that is made of glass, that is made of gold, and he said, "Thank you. Thank you. The spare's a promise. The spare says I expect you to come loose, and I expect to fix you, and I am not going anywhere."

And Goldie came, which was a thing she always did, because Goldie came to everything, because Goldie was the kind of neighbor who came to everything, and she brought her pocket, the marble and the paperclip and the rubber band, the three useless things that were loved and were not kept, and she said, "To the captain. To the homecoming. To the love stays." And the pocket jingled the small, satisfied jingle, and the homecoming was a homecoming, and the celebration was a celebration.

And for a little while, for a golden little while, it was enough. For a little while, the captain was home, and the label said HOME, COMES BACK TO, and the family was together, and the Bureau was there, and the buns were eaten, and the loving was the ground under the fog, and it was there the whole time, and it was there now. For a little while, it was enough. For a little while, they had won. They had gotten one thing home. They had answered one heartbreak, a little, just a crack. They had proven that a thing could go home. They had proven that the love stays.

And then Ollie, who was the Official Counter, who therefore looked at things, who therefore looked out the window, said, "Pip. Pip, look. The house. The Snell place. Look."

And Pip looked. And the house had grown a new room.

It was a small thing, at first, a thing you saw the way you see a plant grow, which is to say you saw it if you looked, and if you looked away and looked back it was a little bigger. But this was not a little bigger. This was a new room. A whole new room, on the side of the house, a room that had not been there, a room that the outside of the house did not have room for, a room with a curtained window, a room that was holding its breath. And the hum was louder. The hum was much louder. The hum was a hum you felt in your teeth, in your bones, in the part of you that knows when a thing is too big for the room it is in, and the house was making room, and the Keeper was filling it, and the Keeper was growing.

"It grew," said Pip. "The house. It grew a new room."

"The Keeper grows," said Barnaby, and his whiskers went down, and his voice was the voice of a walrus who has been the Chief for a very long time and has just seen a thing he was afraid of. "The more it keeps, the bigger it gets. And the bigger it gets, the more room it needs. And the house makes room." He looked at the new room, at the curtained window, and he said, "We let one thing go. We let the captain go. And the Keeper… the Keeper compensated. The Keeper kept harder. The Keeper grew. Because… because a machine that keeps does not like to let go. And when it lets one thing go, it keeps everything else harder. It… it makes up for it. It grows."

And Pip, who was eleven, who had a label maker, who had just watched a house grow a new room, felt the ground under the fog go cold, because the homecoming was a homecoming, but the homecoming was also a… the homecoming was also a thing that made the Keeper grow. They had gotten one thing home, and the Keeper had grown. They had answered one heartbreak, a little, just a crack, and the Keeper had grown. They had proven that a thing could go home, and the Keeper had decided to keep everything else harder.

"The false victory," said Miss Whisk, quietly, and her paw went to her empty collar. "We won one thing. And the machine grew. That's… that's the false victory. That's the thing that looks like winning and is actually… is actually the machine getting stronger." She looked at the new room, at the curtained window, and she said, "Tomorrow is Finding Day. And tomorrow, the Keeper is going to keep everything. And the Keeper is bigger now. The Keeper is much bigger now. And tomorrow… tomorrow is going to be harder than we thought."

And the celebration was still a celebration, because the captain was home, because the label said HOME, COMES BACK TO, because the family was together, because the loving was the ground under the fog. But the celebration was also a… the celebration was also a thing with a new room in it, a thing with a hum that was louder, a thing with a Keeper that was bigger, a thing with a tomorrow that was going to be harder than they thought.

And Pip, who was eleven, who had a label maker, who had just watched a house grow a new room, looked at the Bureau, at the walrus and the cat and the hedgehog and the girl, and he said, "Tomorrow. Tomorrow we answer it. Tomorrow we show it the love stays. Tomorrow we… we make it stop. We have to. We have to make it stop. Because if we don't… if we don't, the Keeper is going to keep everything. And the town is going to forget. And the things are going to be cold. And the almost is going to be everything. We have to make it stop."

"Tomorrow," said Barnaby. "On Finding Day. The best day. And we are going to make it the best day. We are going to answer the heartbreak. We are going to prove that the love stays." He looked at Pip with his small, kind eyes. "Are you ready, Friend of the Lost?"

"I'm ready," said Pip. And he was. He was ready. He had a pocket full of useless junk that was going to help, and a cat with a stamp, and a walrus who says no, and a hedgehog who sorts the socks, and a six-year-old who counts to zero. He was ready. He did not know the how. He did not know the exactly. But he was ready, and being ready was the thing you could do, and the how was the thing that would come, and the how would come because he was ready.

And the house with too many curtains held its breath, and the new room held its breath, and the Keeper hummed, patient and keeping, bigger now, much bigger now, getting ready for Finding Day.

And tomorrow was Finding Day. And tomorrow, they were going to prove that the love stays. And tomorrow, they were going to make the best day the best day.

But tonight, there was a homecoming, and a new room, and a hum that was louder, and a boy who had decided to make a machine stop.

And tonight, that was a start.
