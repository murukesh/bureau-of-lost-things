## Chapter 40 - The Lost-and-Found Picnic

The picnic was the picnic, and the picnic was the bring-a-thing-you-love, and the town came. The town came with its things. The town came with the things it loved. The town came with the music boxes and the telescopes and the harmonicas and the red mittens, the things that had gone home along the sock trail, the things that had been kept and were now warm, the things that had been almost and were now home. And the town sat together, in the park, on the first day of fall, under the bunting and the lights, and the town ate, and the town was together, and the town brought a thing it loved.

And Ollie, who was six, who was the Official Counter, who was brave in small, loud ways, stood on a table, because you stood on a table when you were about to do a thing you had promised, and he held his page, the one that said OFFICIAL COUNTER, OLLIE PARKER, AGE 6, VERY GOOD AT COUNTING, and he said, "I'm going to count to zero. I'm going to count to zero. I promised. I'm the Official Counter. I count to zero. And zero means nothing's missing. And zero is the best number. And I'm going to count to zero."

He counted the things home - the music box, the telescope, the harmonica, the red mitten, the eighteen, the nineteen, the twenty, the everything. He counted them down, and the town counted with him, and the number went down, and down, and down.

And the number got to zero.

"Zero," said Ollie. "Zero. Nothing's missing. Nothing's missing. Zero." And he looked at Pip with his whole face, and he said, "I counted to zero. I counted to zero. I promised. And I did it. I did it. I counted to zero." And he held up the page, and the page said ZERO, and the town cheered, and the town cheered for the Official Counter, and the town cheered for the zero, and the zero was the best number, and the zero was the nothing's missing, and the zero was the home.

And Goldie, who was twelve, who knew every shortcut, who had a pocket that was empty, the marble spent, the paperclip spent, the rubber band spent, the three useless things that had done the job, sat on the grass, and she patted the empty pocket, and the pocket jingled, but the jingle was a different jingle, the jingle of a pocket that has done the job, the jingle of a pocket that is empty and is full. And she reached into the pocket, and she found a thing, a new thing, a thing she had not known was there, a button, a small blue button, four-holed, the kind that holds a coat together, the kind that is a promise. And she held it up, and she said, "Huh. A button. A new button. A new useless thing." And she smiled the shortcut smile and said, "The sad's the good kind. The sad's the good kind. The kind that means it was real. And the pocket... the pocket's got a new thing. The pocket's got a new useless thing. And I love it. And I don't mind when it goes. Those are two different things." And she put the button in the pocket, and the pocket jingled the small, satisfied jingle, and the pocket was full again, and the pocket was the pocket, and the pocket was the love.

And Thimble, who sorted the socks, who gave spare buttons to lost coats, who was prepared for loss, got his red star button back. The coat came home, the coat that had been kept, the coat that had been pulled, the coat that had had the red star button popped off, and the coat came home along the sock trail, and the red star button came home with it, and Thimble held the red star button in his paw, and he said, "There. There. You came back. You came back. I expect you to come loose, and I expect to fix you, and I am not going anywhere. And you came back. And I'm not going anywhere. And the word is let go. And the word is let go. And the word is... the word is home." And he put the red star button back in his quills, and his quills clicked, and the buttons were there, and the red star was there, and the red star was home.

And Miss Whisk, who was the Head Cataloguer, who kept the record of everyone's loving, who had a bell in the back under administrative, stamped the ledger, and the stamp said GOING HOME, and it was the last GOING HOME, and her paw did not shake, and the almost-smile was there, in the corner, permanent now. And she went to the back, to the file, to the BELL. ADMINISTRATIVE., and she left the drawer open a little, so it got some air, so it knew it was not forgotten. And she said, "The love stays. Even when the bell is gone. The love stays. And the bowl... the bowl is left out. The bowl is left out. And that's the job. That's the whole job." And her paw went to her empty collar, and she did not mind, and the not-minding was the good kind, the kind that means it was real.

And Snell came to the picnic. Snell came to the picnic with Biscuit. Biscuit, who was fat, who was happy, who was old, who was different, who was the same, who was Biscuit. Snell sat on the grass, and Biscuit put his head on his foot, just his head, on his foot, the way he used to, the way he always did, and Snell put his long hand on Biscuit's head, and he was not alone. He was not alone. He was not alone. And the town came to him, and the town said, "Silas. Silas Snell. Welcome back. Welcome back. It's been eight years. Welcome back." And Snell said, "Thank you. Thank you. It's... it's good to be back. It's good to be... it's good to be not alone." And the collar tag was in his pocket, and the collar tag was warm, and the collar tag was home.

And the Keeper was an ordinary shed. The Keeper was an ordinary shed, and the town made it a shelter, a place for the picnic, a place for the things, a place for the waiting, and the shed was not a Keeper anymore, the shed was a shed, and the shed was a home. And the house with too many curtains opened the curtains. The house opened the curtains, and the light came in, and the house was not holding its breath anymore, and the house was a house, and the house was a home.

And Pip, who was eleven, who had a label maker, who had come to Marrow's End feeling like a lost thing, who had labeled his shoe STILL HERE because he had not been sure it was true, sat on the grass, and he looked at the town, at the picnic, at the things that were home, at the brother who had counted to zero, at the girl whose pocket was full again, at the hedgehog whose red star was home, at the cat whose almost-smile was permanent, at the walrus who leaked contentedly, at the man shaped like an umbrella who was not alone, at the dog who was fat and happy and different and the same. And he felt the thing go warm in his chest, the rearranging, the furniture-moving, the thing he did not have a label for yet. And he thought: *I'm not a lost thing anymore. I'm found. I'm a keeper. And the love stays - even when the thing is lost, even when it's kept, even when it's gone, even when it comes back differently. The love stays.*

And he thought: *feeling lost is not the same as being unloved. I felt lost, and I was loved the whole time. The loving was the ground under the fog. I just needed to find the Bureau to see it. And I found the Bureau. And I found the home.*

And the picnic was the picnic, and the town was together, and the things were home, and the love stayed, and the zero was the best number, and the first day of fall was the first day of fall, and the Finding Day was the Finding Day, and the best day was the best day.

And Pip, who was eleven, who had a label maker, who had decided on the first night that nothing in this family would ever get lost, who had learned that you can't keep everything you love, who had learned that some things come back differently, who had learned that the love stays, took out the label maker. And he went to the Bureau. He went to the green door, the small green door, the door that was shy, the door that only showed itself to people who had lost something they truly loved, the door that was the way in, and the way out, and the way home. And the door was back in the school wall, where it belonged, where it had been pulled from, where it had come back to. And the door was found. And the door was home.

And Pip printed one last label. And the label was the last label on the roll, the little white tongue of it, the one that had been waiting, the one that had been waiting since the first night, since the STILL HERE, since the FOUND. COMES BACK TO., since the CAPTAIN CHOMP. HOME. And the label was the last label. And the label was the answer. And the label was the love. And the label was the home.

Then Pip printed one last label, pressed it onto the Bureau's door, and stepped back to read it: FOUND.
